OUR IMPACT AMBITION

Communities shaping their own development.

We want to contribute to communities where:

Young people have a voice.

Young people have practical economic opportunities.

Young mothers can build dignified livelihoods.

Families can access nutritious foods.

Communities can produce more of what they consume.

Farmers can explore affordable and climate-smart production systems.

Nutrition knowledge becomes household action.

Community priorities reach policy spaces.

Citizens participate in decisions affecting public resources.

Local innovations become viable enterprises.

Community knowledge becomes a resource for others.

OUR PRACTICAL IMPACT CHAIN

From voice to transformation.

01
VOICE — Youth and communities understand their rights and participate in governance.
02
KNOWLEDGE — People gain nutrition, civic, agricultural and enterprise knowledge.
03
SKILLS — Participants develop practical abilities.
04
ACTION — They apply those skills through community initiatives and demonstrations.
05
ENTERPRISE — Promising ideas become livelihood opportunities.
06
INCOME — Enterprises generate economic value.
07
NUTRITION — Households gain stronger pathways to nutritious food and protein.
08
RESILIENCE — Communities become better equipped to withstand economic, social and environmental pressures.
09
TRANSFORMATION — Communities increasingly shape their own development.
THE JAMII AJIBIKA MODEL

Listen. Organize. Educate. Advocate. Demonstrate. Train. Incubate. Connect. Document. Replicate.

01 — LISTEN

Understand the real community problem.

02 — ORGANIZE

Bring affected people together.

03 — EDUCATE

Build knowledge, leadership and awareness.

04 — ADVOCATE

Create space for community voices in decision-making.

05 — DEMONSTRATE

Put practical solutions into action.

06 — TRAIN

Build technical and enterprise skills.

07 — INCUBATE

Support participants to move from learning to enterprise.

08 — CONNECT

Link people to partners, markets, institutions and opportunities.

09 — DOCUMENT

Capture results, stories, lessons and evidence.

10 — REPLICATE

Take what works to more households and communities.

WHAT WE BELIEVE

Young people are not merely beneficiaries.

They are leaders, innovators, entrepreneurs and community problem-solvers.

Nutrition is not only a health issue.

It is also an economic, agricultural, social and governance issue.

Climate action must make economic sense to communities.

A climate-smart solution should also improve resilience and livelihoods.

Training is not enough.

Skills must have a pathway into practice, enterprise and income.

Advocacy must lead to action.

Community voices should influence decisions, budgets, services and policies.

Local innovation matters.

Communities often possess solutions that need support, refinement, evidence and investment.

OUR BIGGER VISION

Youth leadership, nutrition security, climate-smart livelihoods and accountable governance reinforcing one another.

Jamii Ajibika is working towards a future where youth leadership, nutrition security, climate-smart livelihoods and accountable governance reinforce one another.

We envision a community development model in which a young person can:

find their voice → gain knowledge → develop skills → access opportunity → start an enterprise → earn an income → improve household nutrition → participate in community leadership → mentor another young person.

That is the transformation we want to see.
